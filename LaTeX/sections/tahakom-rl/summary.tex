\section{Summary}
\begin{frame}{Summary}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0.6em
    \item \textbf{The language.} An MDP $(\mathcal{S}, \mathcal{A}, R, P, \gamma)$; a
          policy maps states to actions; the goal is the largest expected discounted
          return.
    \item \textbf{Value-based RL.} Nudge $Q(s,a)$ toward the Bellman target
          $r + \gamma \max_{a'} Q(s',a')$ and act greedily, with some
          $\varepsilon$-exploration. Q-learning needs no model of the environment.
    \item \textbf{Policy-based RL.} Step along
          $\sum_t \hat{A}_t\, \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a_t|s_t)$: make
          better-than-average actions more likely. Baselines and advantages cut the
          variance; PPO clips each step; GRPO takes its baseline from a group of
          attempts.
    \item \textbf{The real world.} Often the reward is the hard part. RLHF learns it
          from comparisons and needs a KL leash against reward hacking; verifiable
          rewards remove the learned judge; robots add safety and the sim-to-real
          gap.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}
