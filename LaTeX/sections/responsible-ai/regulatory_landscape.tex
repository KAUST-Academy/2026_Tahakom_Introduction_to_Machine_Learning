% --- SLIDE 1: THE SAUDI FRAME ---
\begin{frame}{Governance Is Also Law: The Saudi Frame}

    \begin{columns}[T]
        \begin{column}{0.49\textwidth}
            \begin{tcolorbox}[colback=raiBlue!5, colframe=raiBlue!60,
                              title={SDAIA AI Ethics Principles (2023)}]
                \scriptsize
                \textbf{Seven principles:} fairness; privacy and security; humanity; social and
                environmental benefits; reliability and safety; transparency and explainability;
                accountability and responsibility.

                \vspace{0.4em}
                \textbf{Four risk tiers:}
                \begin{itemize}
                    \item \textbf{Little or no risk:} the principles are recommended.
                    \item \textbf{Limited or high risk:} the principles are required, with additional
                          controls for high risk.
                    \item \textbf{Unacceptable risk:} prohibited, for example threats to the safety of
                          life or the exploitation of children.
                \end{itemize}
                \vspace{0.2em}
                Applies to developers, designers and users of AI systems, and to the people they affect.
            \end{tcolorbox}
        \end{column}
        \begin{column}{0.49\textwidth}
            \begin{tcolorbox}[colback=gray!8, colframe=gray!60,
                              title={Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL)}]
                \scriptsize
                \begin{itemize}
                    \item In force since 14 September 2023; enforced by SDAIA since 14 September 2024.
                    \item Training sets, prediction logs and monitoring data are \textbf{personal data}
                          whenever they relate to identifiable people: they need a lawful basis, a stated
                          purpose, and no more data than that purpose requires.
                    \item Breaches are reported to SDAIA within \textbf{72 hours}; data leaves the Kingdom
                          only under its transfer rules.
                    \item Fines up to \textbf{SAR 5 million} per violation, doubled for repeat offences.
                \end{itemize}
            \end{tcolorbox}
        \end{column}
    \end{columns}

    \vspace{0.5em}
    \centering
    \small \textit{This lecture's tools map onto them:} a disaggregated audit is evidence for
    \emph{fairness}; a model card for \emph{transparency} and \emph{accountability}; and the
    prediction-log retention decision in the MLOps lecture later today is a \emph{PDPL} decision.

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{International Reference Points}

    \centering
    \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.35}
    {\scriptsize
    \begin{tabular}{p{2.8cm}|p{4.5cm}|p{4.5cm}}
        \toprule
                                       & \textbf{What it is} & \textbf{When it matters to you} \\
        \midrule
        \textbf{EU AI Act}\newline Regulation (EU) 2024/1689
            & Binding EU law, with risk tiers close to SDAIA's: prohibited practices, high-risk systems,
              transparency duties, minimal risk
            & Any system sold or used in the EU. Prohibitions apply since February 2025; after the 2026
              Digital Omnibus, high-risk duties start in December 2027 (August 2028 for AI inside
              regulated products) \\
        \textbf{NIST AI RMF 1.0}\newline (2023)
            & Voluntary US framework built on four functions: Govern, Map, Measure, Manage
            & A ready-made structure for an internal AI risk process \\
        \textbf{ISO/IEC 42001:2023}
            & Certifiable management-system standard for AI
            & Showing a customer or regulator that the process exists and is audited \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}}

    \vspace{0.6em}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{The common core is this lecture:} know the risk tier, test for disparate harm,
              document intended and out-of-scope use, keep a named human accountable, and monitor
              after release.
        \item \textbf{Dates move.} These are as of October 2026; check the current text before you rely on
              one.
    \end{itemize}

\end{frame}
