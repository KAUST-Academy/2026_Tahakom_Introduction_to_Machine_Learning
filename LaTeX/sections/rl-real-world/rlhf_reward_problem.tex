\section{Learning Rewards from Feedback: RLHF}

% ================================================================ 3.1a  The wall
\begin{frame}{First, a Reward We Cannot Write Down}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0.5em
    \item Every method so far assumed a reward function $r$ we could
          \emph{write down}: the game score, $-$distance to a target, $+1$ for a
          win.
    \item Now suppose the goal is \empr{``be a helpful, honest, harmless
          assistant.''} What number measures that? There is no formula.
\end{itemize}
\vspace{0.5em}
\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.5\textwidth}\centering
\setlength{\fboxsep}{6pt}%
\colorbox{boxgreen!50}{\parbox{0.92\linewidth}{\footnotesize
\textbf{Answer A.} ``It's the force that pulls things down. It's why a ball
falls when you let go of it.''}}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.5\textwidth}\centering
\setlength{\fboxsep}{6pt}%
\colorbox{boxred!40}{\parbox{0.92\linewidth}{\footnotesize
\textbf{Answer B.} ``Gravity is the curvature of spacetime sourced by the
stress--energy tensor, $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$\dots''}}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\vspace{0.5em}
\begin{center}\small
\emph{Prompt: ``Explain gravity to a 6-year-old.''}\\
Almost everyone prefers \textbf{A}, yet \empr{no one can write the formula that
says so.}
\end{center}
\end{frame}

% ================================================================ 3.1b  The idea
\begin{frame}{The Idea: Don't Write the Reward, Learn It}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0.45em
    \item \empr{When you cannot write the reward, recover it from data.} This is
          called \emph{inverse RL}, and RLHF is its most consequential example.
    \item People are bad at giving a \emph{number} (``this answer is a 7.3'') but
          good at \emph{comparing}: ``A is better than B.'' Comparisons are cheap
          and consistent.
    \item \textbf{The plan, in three moves:}
    \begin{enumerate}\footnotesize\itemsep0.2em
        \item Collect many human \emph{comparisons} of model answers.
        \item Train a model to predict those comparisons; this learned model
              \emph{becomes our reward signal}.
        \item Use RL to push the policy toward answers that score well under it.
    \end{enumerate}
    \item This is \textbf{Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF)}, the
          method behind ChatGPT-style assistants.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}
